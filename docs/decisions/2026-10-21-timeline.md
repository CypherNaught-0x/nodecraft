## 2026-10-21 · The Timeline stores clips on its node and renders with the settings' ffmpeg (#109)

- **Clips live in the node's config.** `video.timeline` keeps its clips as one JSON document
  under `config["nodecraft.timeline"]`, changed only through `node.timeline_set` and the
  `timeline.*` commands, so every edit is one invertible changeset. The output frame (width,
  height, frame rate, background) is made of ordinary parameters.
- **Wired videos are clips by identity, as Layer Editor layers are.** Each wired video is listed
  as a whole-video clip whose id comes from its upstream node and its index there, until it is
  edited; then it is stored. A stored clip whose wire is gone stays listed but doesn't play until
  it is deleted or wired in again. A video can play more than once: `timeline.split` stores a
  second clip of the same video. A wired video's only clip can't be deleted (it would come back
  as an automatic clip), only disabled.
- **One track, in microseconds.** Clips play one after another; `transition_us` is the cross-fade
  into the next clip (0 cuts), and the last clip's is ignored. Times are microseconds into the
  clip's own video. Captions, stills, more audio tracks, clip geometry and speed are follow-ups,
  not hidden fields.
- **Rendering is a local action that uses the shared video service.** The executor imports each
  connected video into `nodecraft-media`'s video service, checks every clip against the probed
  length, composes one WebM (VP9 and Opus) and stages it for import. The tools are the absolute
  `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` paths in the settings' `[media]` section, never a project's; without
  them, and in the browser, the run fails with an error that says what to do.
- **It runs when asked.** A render can take minutes, so live recompute skips the Timeline and the
  source runner gives a long action (`ActionExecutor::long_running`) a thread of its own instead
  of one of the run runtime's workers. Cancelling the run stops the render.
- **Bounded on the way in and out.** At most 32 clips, an hour of output, 256 MiB per connected
  video and 256 MiB rendered; the rendered video goes into the project through the streaming
  asset import, since it can exceed the resident asset cap.
